AI Business Operating System
Master Architecture, Product Scope & Codex Implementation Specification
Internal production system • Contabo VPS • Open-source-first • October 2026
Purpose: define the agreed product scope, architecture, integration boundaries, implementation priorities, verification gates, and delivery criteria for building an internal AI-powered business operating system. This specification intentionally favors reuse of mature open-source components over rebuilding capabilities.
1. Executive Architecture Decision
Twenty CRM is the authoritative system of record for customers, companies, opportunities, pipelines, business objects, permissions, workflows, and cross-functional business state.
Ringee is the calling subsystem and must be evaluated as three coordinated repositories/components: Ringee App, Ringee Voice, and Ringee Mobile.
Ringee's existing telephony, AI voice, and calling orchestration capabilities must be inspected and reused first. Do not build a parallel voice stack unless a verified gap requires it.
Mautic is the marketing automation subsystem for segmentation, journeys, newsletters, analytics, and future guarded AI-driven campaign operations.
AI orchestration is layered: use native Twenty and Ringee orchestration where appropriate; add a cross-system orchestrator only for capabilities that cannot be cleanly expressed in the native systems.
n8n is optional, not a mandatory core dependency. Use it only when native workflows/agents cannot satisfy an integration cleanly.
2. Guiding Principles
Reuse before rebuild:  Fork, integrate, configure, or extend existing open-source code before creating a new service.
One source of truth:  Avoid competing customer databases. Twenty owns canonical customer/business state; operational subsystems may retain the minimum state they require.
Verify source, not marketing:  Commercial website claims are discovery signals only. Codex must confirm self-hosted source-code support, configuration, licensing, and runtime behavior before relying on a feature.
Modular boundaries:  Twenty, Ringee, Mautic, telephony, AI providers, email, calendar, invoicing, and analytics must remain replaceable through documented APIs/events.
Least-cost intelligence:  Route tasks to the least expensive model that meets quality and latency requirements; reserve premium models for complex reasoning.
Human control where consequential:  High-impact external actions such as ad spend, sensitive communications, destructive changes, and payment operations require configurable guardrails/approvals.

4. Ringee Subsystem - Confirmed Scope
Ringee is not a single-repository decision. Codex must treat the following repositories as a coordinated subsystem and map their APIs, shared schemas, authentication, deployment topology, dependencies, and version compatibility.
Ringee App:  Primary web/calling application. Inspect contacts/leads, campaigns, sessions, dispositions, notes, callbacks, recordings, transcripts, analytics, integrations, AI features, and administration.
Ringee Voice:  Primary candidate for the telephony/voice engine. Inspect the Asterisk relationship/fork changes, SIP/provider integration, call routing, recording, media handling, AI voice hooks, concurrency, scaling, and observability.
Ringee Mobile:  Mobile companion. Customize branding and backend endpoints; preserve human business calling. Produce an installable Android APK for internal use. Keep an AAB path available for future Play Store distribution.
5. Ringee Voice & AI - Build-vs-Reuse Gate
Before introducing LiveKit, a separate STT service, TTS service, custom media bridge, or a new call orchestrator, Codex must perform a capability audit of Ringee.
Determine which autonomous AI voice-agent features exist in the self-hosted source versus only in Ringee's hosted/commercial service.
Verify outbound and inbound autonomous calling separately.
Verify configurable STT, LLM, TTS, knowledge/context retrieval, interruption/barge-in, tool calling, appointment booking, structured outcomes, summaries, transcripts, recordings, and call transfer/escalation.
Verify bring-your-own model/provider keys and whether provider selection can be controlled per workspace, campaign, or agent.
Verify MCP/API surfaces for starting calls, creating campaigns/sessions, retrieving outcomes/transcripts, and scheduling follow-ups.
If Ringee already implements a capability satisfactorily, reuse it. If partially implemented, extend it. Only build a replacement when the gap is documented with evidence.
6. CRM Data Ownership & Synchronization
Twenty remains the canonical CRM. Ringee may maintain the operational records necessary for calls, but it must not become a second independent master customer database.
Twenty -> Ringee: contact identity, phone numbers, company/account context, campaign eligibility, owner, consent/do-not-call status, relevant CRM fields, and selected knowledge/context.
Ringee -> Twenty: call attempts, timestamps, agent/campaign, disposition, transcript reference, recording reference, summary, sentiment/structured outcome where available, callback, meeting/appointment, next action, and failure reason.
Use stable cross-system IDs and idempotent synchronization.
Define conflict rules. Canonical CRM fields are resolved in Twenty unless a field is explicitly delegated.
Avoid copying sensitive data unless required by the downstream function.
7. Pipelines & Customer Lifecycle
Twenty must support configurable pipelines/stages for sales and service processes. Ringee calling activity attaches to the same customer/opportunity history rather than creating a separate lifecycle.
Lead -> discovery -> audit/qualification -> proposal -> follow-up -> closed won/lost, with configurable/skippable stages.
Separate pipelines/workflows may be created for website projects, retainer clients, SaaS sales, or future business units.
Inbound and outbound calling must resolve to the same customer identity and timeline.
AI-generated next actions must be visible, attributable, auditable, and permission-controlled.
8. Multi-Workspace / Tenant Separation
Multiple workspaces/clients/campaigns are a mandatory product requirement. Do not immediately build custom tenancy into Ringee; first audit its native organization/workspace/team/campaign isolation.
Map native Ringee organization/workspace capabilities and data boundaries.
Map Twenty workspace/permission boundaries.
Ensure contacts, campaigns, phone numbers, recordings, transcripts, knowledge, credentials, AI agents, and analytics can be isolated appropriately.
If native isolation is insufficient, design the smallest extension that preserves upstream compatibility.
9. RBAC and AI-Agent Identities
RBAC must apply across CRM, calling, campaigns, recordings, transcripts, marketing, invoicing, integrations, secrets, AI configuration, and administrative actions.
AI agents are first-class identities. They receive explicit roles/scopes just like users or service accounts.
An AI agent may receive broad/full access only when deliberately granted; default to least privilege.
All AI actions must carry actor identity, workspace, tool/action, timestamp, target object, result, and approval context where applicable.
Secrets/API keys must never be exposed in ordinary logs or model prompts.
10. AI Orchestration Model
Use orchestration closest to the domain. Ringee handles call-domain orchestration when its native implementation is sufficient. Twenty handles CRM/business workflows and agents when sufficient. A higher-level orchestrator coordinates cross-domain goals only where needed.
Example: Twenty identifies overdue sales follow-up -> Ringee initiates approved calling campaign -> Ringee returns outcome -> Twenty updates opportunity -> Mautic adjusts nurture segment.
Cross-system orchestration must use APIs/events/MCP rather than direct database coupling wherever practical.
Agents need tool registries, scoped credentials, retry/error policies, budgets, rate limits, and human approval gates.
Store concise task state and structured outputs rather than unnecessarily retaining entire model conversations.
11. Mautic Marketing Scope
Visual email/newsletter authoring.
Segments and dynamic audience membership.
Drip/nurture journeys and event-driven campaigns.
Delivery through Amazon SES or another pluggable provider.
Open/click/bounce/unsubscribe analytics and campaign performance.
CRM-triggered enrollment and outcome synchronization with Twenty.
Future AI-assisted content, audience, and campaign optimization.
Future ad-platform automation must include spend limits, account/workspace isolation, approval thresholds, and rollback/stop controls.
12. Inbound & Outbound Calling Requirements
Human outbound calls from web and mobile.
Human inbound call handling where supported by the telephony configuration.
Autonomous outbound AI calls where self-hosted Ringee supports them.
Autonomous inbound AI calls if verified; otherwise document the missing layer and implement only what is necessary.
Campaign lists, schedules, retries, time-zone/contact-window rules, suppression lists, dispositions, callbacks, transfers/escalations, recordings, transcripts, summaries, and analytics.
Shared customer history across inbound/outbound and human/AI interactions.
13. Ringee Mobile Deliverable
Fork/customize the mobile codebase rather than writing a new app unless source quality makes reuse impractical.
Connect to the internal/self-hosted backend and authentication model.
Support business calling, contacts, call history, notes/outcomes, callbacks, and other capabilities already supported by the upstream app after verification.
Add AI monitoring/management features only after determining what the upstream mobile app and APIs expose.
Produce a signed or appropriately installable internal Android APK and documented build steps. Keep signing secrets outside source control.
Document the path to an Android App Bundle (AAB) for future store distribution.
14. Email, Calendar, Invoicing & Business Operations
Provide integration points for business email and calendars so agents/workflows can draft, schedule, follow up, and coordinate meetings subject to permissions.
Support invoicing/payment workflows through pluggable providers (for example Stripe or accounting integrations) without making one provider the architectural core.
Customer timelines should surface relevant communications, meetings, invoices/payment state, marketing interactions, and calls.
External actions must be attributable and auditable; sensitive actions can require approval.
15. MCP, APIs & Integration Contract
Inventory every existing API, webhook, MCP server/tool, event bus, and integration surface in Twenty, Ringee, and Mautic.
Prefer supported extension points over database writes or invasive forks.
Create a canonical internal event vocabulary for customer.updated, opportunity.stage_changed, call.completed, call.failed, meeting.booked, campaign.enrolled, invoice.overdue, etc.
Implement idempotency keys, retries with backoff, dead-letter handling, correlation IDs, and observable failure states.
Document authentication and authorization for every integration path.
16. Deployment on Contabo
Containerize services where practical and separate application, database, cache/queue, media/telephony, and reverse-proxy responsibilities.
Use environment-specific secrets management; never commit credentials.
TLS for all web/API endpoints; secure SIP/media configuration appropriate to the selected telephony providers.
Persistent volumes and tested backup/restore for databases, configuration, recordings where retained, and critical application state.
Health checks, structured logs, metrics, resource monitoring, restart policies, and alerting.
Document ports, DNS/subdomain assumptions, firewall requirements, and deployment order.
17. Security, Privacy & Compliance Engineering
Consent, do-not-call, unsubscribe, retention, and recording controls must be configurable by jurisdiction/business policy.
Encrypt sensitive data in transit and at rest where appropriate.
Use short-lived/scoped credentials where possible.
Audit privileged user and agent actions.
Provide configurable recording/transcript retention and deletion.
Do not assume legal permission to record or auto-dial; deployment configuration must support applicable local requirements.
18. Cost Control & Model Routing
Track cost per call, minute, transcription, synthesis, LLM request, campaign, workspace, and agent where data is available.
Support model/provider abstraction so lower-cost models can handle classification, summarization, extraction, and routine responses.
Use premium reasoning models only for tasks that materially benefit.
Cache stable knowledge/context and avoid repeatedly sending large CRM histories.
Set per-agent/workspace budgets and rate limits.
19. Licensing & Fork Management
Licenses must be verified from each repository at the exact pinned revision before modification or redistribution. Do not assume that Ringee App, Voice, and Mobile share identical licensing.
Record repository URL, upstream owner, default branch, pinned commit/tag, license file, SPDX identifier if available, and obligations.
Maintain upstream remotes and document the update/rebase strategy.
Keep custom changes isolated where practical to reduce merge conflicts.
Track notices/source-disclosure obligations for any distributed APK or other binaries.
Perform the same license inventory for Twenty, Mautic, and added dependencies.
20. Codex Discovery Phase - Mandatory Before Major Coding
Clone/fork or otherwise establish controlled working copies of the three Ringee repositories, Twenty, and Mautic as applicable.
Generate dependency and service maps.
Identify authentication, workspace, RBAC, API, webhook, MCP, AI, telephony, mobile, database, and deployment modules.
Build a feature matrix with statuses: confirmed in self-hosted source / hosted-only / partial / absent / unknown.
Document all gaps against this specification.
Propose the minimum-change implementation plan, prioritizing configuration and extensions before forks and new services.
Do not begin a replacement voice/orchestration implementation until the Ringee capability audit is complete.
21. Implementation Phases
Phase 0 - Audit & pin:  Licenses, repositories, versions, deployment dependencies, capability matrix, architecture decision records.
Phase 1 - Core platform:  Deploy Twenty, Ringee subsystem, Mautic, databases/queues, reverse proxy, secrets, backups, observability.
Phase 2 - Identity & data:  SSO/auth where applicable, workspace model, RBAC, canonical IDs, Twenty-Ringee synchronization.
Phase 3 - Human calling:  Web/mobile business calling, customer history, outcomes, recordings/transcripts as supported.
Phase 4 - AI calling:  Enable/extend Ringee native AI voice and calling orchestration; add only verified missing pieces.
Phase 5 - Marketing:  Mautic segmentation, email journeys, SES/provider integration, CRM sync and analytics.
Phase 6 - Cross-system agents:  Twenty/business agents, MCP/tool registry, cross-domain orchestration, approvals and budgets.
Phase 7 - Business ops:  Email/calendar/invoicing integrations, richer dashboards, automated follow-up.
Phase 8 - Advanced autonomy:  Guarded ad operations, optimization loops, additional AI agents, optional customer-facing expansion.
22. Acceptance Criteria
A customer created/updated in Twenty can be made available to Ringee without duplicate manual entry.
A human can make a business call from supported Ringee interfaces and the result is reflected in the canonical customer timeline.
An AI call can be initiated through the approved orchestration path if the self-hosted capability is verified; its structured outcome returns to Twenty.
Inbound/outbound activity resolves to a common customer identity.
Recordings/transcripts are permission-controlled and retention-configurable.
Workspaces isolate customer data, campaigns, credentials, agents, recordings/transcripts, and analytics to the designed boundary.
AI agents cannot exceed assigned permissions or configured budgets.
Mautic can enroll CRM contacts in a journey and return meaningful engagement state.
Android APK builds reproducibly from documented source/configuration and connects to the internal backend.
Backup/restore, restart/recovery, logging, metrics, and basic security controls are tested.
No major custom service duplicates a capability already verified as satisfactory in the upstream components without an explicit architecture decision record.
23. Explicit Non-Goals for Initial Release
Building a new CRM from scratch.
Building a new PBX/telephony engine when Ringee Voice satisfies the requirement.
Building a new mobile app from zero when Ringee Mobile is reusable.
Building a parallel calling orchestrator when Ringee already satisfies the use case.
Making n8n a mandatory dependency.
Launching a public multi-tenant SaaS product in the first internal release.
Unsupervised high-risk spending or destructive actions without configurable controls.
24. Open Verification Items
These are not product indecision; they are source-code verification tasks that determine the smallest implementation.
Exact self-hosted Ringee AI voice feature set and hosted-only differences.
Exact Ringee Voice modifications relative to upstream Asterisk and how Ringee App integrates with it.
Native Ringee organization/workspace isolation and whether it meets the mandatory multi-workspace requirement.
Ringee Mobile API compatibility with the chosen self-hosted deployment and authentication.
Native MCP/API operations exposed by the self-hosted Ringee build.
Per-repository licenses and redistribution obligations, especially for customized mobile binaries.
Which Twenty agent/workflow capabilities eliminate the need for an additional orchestrator or n8n.
25. Final Direction to Codex
Build this as an integrated operating system, not a collection of disconnected demos. Twenty owns business truth; Ringee owns calling execution; Mautic owns marketing execution. Reuse native AI, workflow, telephony, mobile, API, and MCP capabilities before adding infrastructure. Every new service must have a documented gap that justifies its existence.
